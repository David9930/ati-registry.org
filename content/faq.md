The ATI Registry is a free, public, self-declared registry where authors and publishers state how AI was or was not used in a book. These answers cover the registry itself and the wider questions authors, readers and publishers ask about AI and book writing. They are general information, not legal advice.

## The basics

### What is the ATI Registry?
It is a public registry where an author or publisher declares how generative AI was or was not used in a book. Each declaration receives a permanent record ID and one of [three labels](/labels/), and anyone can [find a record](/registry/) by ID, ISBN, title or author.

### Is the registry a certification or an award?
No. It is not a certification, an endorsement or an award. It records what the declarant says. The label colours (blue, silver and purple) deliberately avoid gold so that a label is not mistaken for a prize.

### Are the declarations verified?
No. Every record is self-declared and the registry does not check manuscripts, compare texts or run detection tools. A record tells you what the author or publisher stated, nothing more. Its credibility comes from the statement being public, dated, permanent and open to challenge (see [Disputes and corrections](/disputes/)).

### Who runs the registry?
It is a founder-led pilot, and the site says so. The founder intends to hand it to an independent steward; see [Governance](/governance/) for where things stand and what is intended.

### Does it cost anything?
No. Registration is free for authors and publishers, and so is looking up a record.

### Why would an author register a book?
Common reasons: to answer reader questions about AI use in advance, to have a stable public statement to point to if the question is raised later, to be consistent across a catalogue, or to support a personal or professional commitment to transparency. Registering is voluntary.

### Why would a reader or reviewer check a record?
To see how the author described the work, to compare it with their own expectations, or to decide how to review, recommend or shelve a title. A record is one input, not a verdict.

## Should I disclose AI use?

### Do authors have to disclose AI use?
There is no general rule that applies to every book. Requirements depend on where you publish, who publishes you, what contests or grants you enter and, in some situations, copyright registration. See the legal, platform and professional questions below. Many authors disclose voluntarily because readers increasingly ask.

### Should authors disclose AI use, or is it better to keep it private?
Reasonable people disagree. The main positions are these.

Disclosure advocates argue that readers choose books partly on how they were made, that silence invites suspicion, and that an early, honest statement is easier to defend than an explanation after an accusation.

Privacy advocates argue that readers do not ask whether an author used a spell checker, an editor or a thesaurus, that the line between acceptable and unacceptable tools is contested, and that a public label can invite judgment rather than understanding.

Purists argue that because readers cannot tell by looking, the honest default is to say so, and that books made without AI deserve a credible way to say that too.

The registry takes no side on what you should do. It exists for authors who choose to declare.

### What happens if someone claims I used AI to write my book?
Accusations arrive in several forms: reviews, social media posts, a platform complaint, a contest committee or a publisher inquiry. A practical approach is to stay factual, describe your actual process, and point to any contemporaneous evidence you kept, such as drafts, version history, notes and correspondence with editors. A registry record made before any dispute shows what you said at the time. It does not prove the statement is true and will not by itself resolve a dispute, because the registry does not verify.

### Can AI detectors prove that a book was written by AI?
No. Published research and widespread reports show that detection tools produce both false positives and false negatives, and they can flag human writing, especially by non-native English writers or in plainly structured prose. Treat a detector score as a weak signal at best. It is not proof either way.

### What if I am falsely accused of using AI?
Keep records of your process, respond calmly and specifically, and ask the person or platform what evidence they are relying on. A pre-existing declaration, saved drafts and a documented timeline are the most useful things to have. If the accusation seriously affects a contract, contest or your reputation, consider speaking to a lawyer.

### What if I disclose AI use and readers react badly?
That risk is real. Some readers and communities are hostile to any AI involvement, and others are indifferent. The registry's tiered labels exist so that light, author-directed use of a tool is not described the same way as machine-originated text. Being precise about what you did is the best protection against being judged for something you did not do.

### Can I register a book after it has been published?
Yes. Records can be created for existing books, including older titles. Every record shows the date it was registered, so a declaration made years after publication is distinguishable from one made at launch.

### Can a reader or reviewer register a book they think used AI?
No. Under the [Terms](/terms/), you may register a work only if you are its author or publisher, or have the authority of one of them. If you believe a record is inaccurate, you can [report it](/disputes/) instead.

## Choosing a label

### What are the three labels?
Human Authored (HA), AI Master Edited (AME) and AI Co-Authored (ACA). Cover art, interior illustrations, audio narration and translation are declared separately as human-made, AI-assisted or AI-generated, or not applicable. The full definitions are on the [labels page](/labels/).

### What does Human Authored mean?
The author originated and wrote all of the text. No generative AI wrote, rewrote, translated or expanded any of it, and no wording, passage or plot point that an AI generated or suggested appears in it.

### What does AI Master Edited mean?
The story, ideas and structure come from the human author, who directed the AI and approved every change. The tagline is "Author directed · Author approved." The test is origination: if the AI originated passages from a prompt, those passages are AI-generated even if the task was called editing.

### What does AI Co-Authored mean?
Human and AI materially collaborated on the expressive content. The AI originated new passages, scenes or dialogue, and a human directed, selected and edited the result.

### I used AI only for brainstorming and did not use any of its suggestions. Can I still claim Human Authored?
Under the v0.1 definition, yes: research and brainstorming with AI are allowed only if nothing the AI generated or suggested is used. The difficulty is being honest about "nothing suggested." If an AI-suggested idea, name, title or plot point shaped the book, the label does not fit.

### I used AI to research facts and then wrote everything myself. Which label?
Human Authored is possible if no AI-generated or AI-suggested wording or ideas appear in the text. Facts you verified and then expressed in your own words are different from text or plot points the AI supplied.

### I used a spell checker and grammar tool. Does that count as AI use?
Conventional spelling and basic grammar checking is generally not treated as generative AI. Tools that rewrite sentences, suggest alternative phrasing or change tone are different, and if you accepted their wording, the Human Authored definition no longer fits. When unsure, read the full definition on the [labels page](/labels/) and choose the label you could comfortably defend.

### An AI tool suggested a single word or phrase and I accepted it. Is the book still Human Authored?
Strictly, the definition excludes any wording that an AI generated or suggested. Many authors will find that strict. If the accepted suggestions were more than trivial, AI Master Edited is the more accurate label.

### I wrote a draft and asked AI to polish it, and I approved every change. Which label?
That is the situation AI Master Edited was designed for: the story and structure are yours, and you directed and approved each change.

### I asked AI to write a chapter from my outline and then rewrote it heavily. Which label?
If the AI originated the passage from a prompt, it counts as AI-generated even after you revise it. Depending on how much of the book was produced this way, AI Co-Authored is likely the accurate label.

### I started with an AI-generated draft and rewrote everything in my own words. Which label?
It depends on whether anything the AI generated or suggested, including structure, scenes and wording, survives in the final text. If it does, Human Authored does not apply. If you used the draft only as a rough reference and nothing carried over, you must judge honestly. When in doubt, choose the label that describes more AI involvement.

### I used AI to translate my book. How do I declare that?
Translation is declared separately as human-made, AI-assisted or AI-generated. AI translation also affects the label for the text, because a translation that an AI produced is not Human Authored under the definition.

### I used AI to write my blurb, back-cover copy or marketing text. Does that affect the label?
The labels concern the book's text. Marketing copy that is not part of the book is outside them. Many authors mention it anyway in their plain-language statement. Keep in mind that everything you enter in the form is public.

### I used AI for my cover. How do I declare it?
Cover art is declared separately. The options are human-made, AI-assisted (human-made, with AI used to retouch, upscale, clean up or modify it) and AI-generated (an AI tool created it and a human directed and selected it). A cover created with an AI image tool is AI-generated. Crediting the tool on your copyright page is good practice.

### I used AI-generated or AI-assisted illustrations inside the book. How do I declare them?
Use the separate illustrations declaration, with the same options as for covers, or "not applicable" if the book has no illustrations.

### My audiobook narration is AI-voiced. How do I declare it?
Use the narration declaration. A synthetic voice reading human-written text is a different matter from AI-written text, and the registry keeps the two apart.

### I co-wrote with another human who used AI. Whose declaration applies?
The record describes the book. If any contributor used AI in a way that affects the text, the label should reflect the book as a whole. Coordinate with your co-author before registering.

### I hired a ghostwriter or editor. How do I know whether they used AI?
Ask them and, where possible, put it in your contract. You are the declarant, so you are responsible for the accuracy of what you declare.

### My book is part of a series. Do I register each book?
Each book is a separate record, since AI use can differ between volumes. Registering a series consistently makes the catalogue easier to understand.

### What if I cannot decide between two labels?
Choose the one that describes more AI involvement, and explain your process in the plain-language statement. Over-declaring is less risky than under-declaring.

### Do I have to name the AI tools I used?
The form asks you to list the AI tools used, with the provider, plan or tier, and what each was used for. Being specific helps readers understand your declaration.

## Putting your label to use

### Where do I show the label and ID?
Put the label and record ID on your copyright page or store listing, and link readers to the record. You can download the marks from the [marks page](/marks/).

### What might a copyright-page statement look like?
Something short and factual works best, for example: "AI use declaration: text, AI Master Edited; cover art, AI-generated (tool name). ATI record ATI-YYYY-NNNNNN-XXXX. Self-declared, not verified." Replace the placeholders with your own details and tool names.

### Are there rules for using the marks?
Yes. By registering, you agree to display a label only for a work you have registered and declared accurately, to keep its wording as published, and not to suggest that the registry certified, endorsed or verified your work. See the [Terms](/terms/).

## Purists and other perspectives

### Why do some authors insist on no AI at all?
Purists give several reasons: they see authorship as the act of thinking and writing sentences, so outsourcing that act changes what the work is; they object to how generative models were trained, often on writers' work without permission or payment; they worry about a flood of machine-made books crowding out human writers; and they want readers to be able to trust that a human voice is the only voice on the page. The Human Authored label exists in part to give these authors a way to say so.

### Isn't any use of generative AI a compromise of authorship?
Purists say yes, or at least that the difference between a tool that fixes spelling and a tool that supplies sentences is a difference in kind. Others say AI is simply another writing tool, like a word processor or a thesaurus, and that authorship lies in the judgment applied to the output. The registry's labels do not settle this. They let authors describe what they did so that readers can apply their own standards.

### Is using AI to write a book cheating?
It depends on the standard being applied. A contest with a no-AI rule treats it as a breach. A publisher whose contract requires original human-written text may treat it as a breach of warranty. Many readers and authors regard disclosed, author-directed use as a legitimate craft choice, and others regard any machine-originated text as incompatible with authorship. The common ground is that undisclosed use, where a rule or reasonable expectation requires disclosure, is what most people object to.

### Do purists think AI use should always be disclosed?
Many do, and some argue disclosure should be mandatory. Their reasoning is that readers are making a purchase and a values choice at once and cannot see the process. Opponents of mandatory disclosure respond that rules are hard to define and enforce and that voluntary norms are more flexible. The registry is voluntary.

### Why does the Human Authored definition exclude AI-suggested ideas, not just AI-written text?
Because for a purist, an idea an AI supplied is still an AI contribution to the book. The v0.1 definition takes that strict position deliberately so that the label means something firm. The definitions are drafts, and the strictness of this one is an open policy question.

### Is it fair that books using AI get a different label from books that do not?
Purists say fairness requires accurate description, not identical treatment. Others say separate labels can stigmatise acceptable tool use. The registry's three tiers, and the rule that the test is origination rather than effort or intent, are an attempt to describe differences without ranking them as good and bad.

### Do readers actually care whether a book used AI?
Surveys and online communities show a wide range of views, from strong objection to complete indifference, and attitudes vary by genre and by how AI was used. The registry does not claim a single reader view. It lets readers who care find an answer.

### What do critics of the purist position say?
They argue that a strict "no AI" standard is hard to define as tools blend into ordinary software, that it may disadvantage authors who rely on assistive technology, and that a book should be judged by its quality rather than its process. Purists reply that process is part of what a reader is buying and that accessibility needs can be handled by clear, separate declarations.

### Can a Human Authored claim be trusted if it is not verified?
Not automatically, and the registry says so. The claim is a public statement with the author's name attached, it is dated, and anyone can challenge it. Purists tend to value that accountability, and sceptics point out that anyone can make a false claim. Readers should treat a record as a statement of the author's position, much as they treat any other author's statement.

### Are there other human-authored labels?
Yes. Other organisations have launched their own human-authorship marks, including the Authors Guild's Human Authored programme. The ATI Registry is a separate effort and is not a certification.

## Legal, platform and professional questions

### Does AI use affect copyright?
It can. In the United States, the Copyright Office has said that copyright protects human authorship, that material generated by AI without sufficient human creative control is not protected, and that applicants should disclose more than minimal AI-generated content when registering. Rules differ by country and are still developing. Check current guidance and consult a qualified lawyer about your situation. The registry does not make claims about copyright.

### Do platforms like Amazon KDP, retailers and distributors require AI disclosure?
Several major platforms ask publishers to disclose AI-generated content when publishing, and some distinguish between AI-generated and AI-assisted work. Policies change often, so check each platform's current terms. A registry record does not replace any platform's own disclosure.

### Do traditional publishers require disclosure?
Many contracts now include clauses about AI use, warranties of originality and disclosure duties. Read your contract and ask your publisher if it is unclear.

### Do literary awards and grants require disclosure?
Some do, and some prohibit AI-generated work entirely. Check each competition's rules before entering. Rules are being updated frequently.

### Do the terms of AI tools affect how I can use their output?
They can. When you register, you confirm that you have checked that the terms of each AI tool you list permit use of its output in your publication. The registry does not check this and gives no legal advice on it.

### Can a false declaration create legal liability?
Possibly, depending on the circumstances, for example if a false statement is used to obtain a contract, a grant or an award. This is a legal question and the registry cannot advise on it. Declare accurately.

### Does the registry protect me if I am accused?
No. It provides a dated public statement. It is not proof of authorship or of process.

## Registering and managing a record

### How do I register a book?
Use the [registration form](/register/). It takes about ten minutes. You will need an email address you can open right away, because the registry sends one message with a confirmation link that is valid for 48 hours. When you confirm, the record is created and you receive a record ID and a private link for managing it.

### What information does the form ask for?
Your role (author, publisher or an authorized representative of either), the title, the author name as it appears on the book and the year. The ISBN, edition and a link to the book's page are optional. You then choose the label for the text, write a plain-language statement of how AI was or was not used, list any AI tools used, and declare how cover art, illustrations, narration and translation were made. You can also say which formats the book is available in, such as ebook, print or audiobook.

### What do I confirm when I register?
Four things: that the information is accurate and complete to the best of your knowledge; that you have the right to make the declaration and have checked that each AI tool's terms permit use of its output in your publication; that you understand the declaration is self-declared, not verified and not a certification; and that you agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy, including that the record is published publicly and may later be released as open data under CC0 1.0.

### What does the record ID look like?
IDs follow the pattern ATI-YYYY-NNNNNN-XXXX: a year, a sequence number and a short suffix. IDs are never reused.

### What is the private management link?
It is a private link sent to you when your registration is confirmed. Anyone who holds it can withdraw or correct that record, so keep it private. If you lose it, you can [request a new one](/manage/lost) by email, which stops the old one working.

### Can I change my declaration later?
Yes. Use your private link to correct the declaration or withdraw it. Corrections are recorded in the record's history, so a correction is visible rather than silent.

### Can I delete my record?
Records are meant to be permanent, like an ISBN record. You can withdraw one, and a withdrawn record stays visible with the status "withdrawn." The registry itself may remove a record that is inaccurate, abusive, infringing or an impersonation. A removed record shows only its ID, status and dates. Copies made by others cannot be recalled, so do not register anything you are not comfortable having public.

### How are revised or new editions handled?
The form has an optional edition field. How revised editions are treated is an open policy question during the pilot. If a new edition changes how AI was used, write to [hello@ati-registry.org](mailto:hello@ati-registry.org).

### Will the label definitions change?
They are version 0.1 drafts and are expected to evolve. Each record shows the version of the definitions it was declared under, and the intention is that definitions change only through a public, versioned process.

### Can publishers register books on behalf of authors?
Yes. The form accepts publishers and authorized representatives as well as authors. The declarant is responsible for accuracy, so a publisher should confirm the facts with the author first.

### Can I register an audiobook, print book or ebook?
Yes. The form lets you say which formats the book is available in. The record is for the book, and narration is declared separately.

### Why is there no public list of every registered book?
The registry offers lookup by ID, ISBN, title or author rather than a complete public listing. The Terms say retrieval may be limited to protect the registry from automated copying.

### How do I look up a book?
Use the [Find a record](/registry/) page and search by ID, ISBN, title or author.

## Reports, disputes and corrections

### How do I report a record I think is inaccurate?
Use the link on the record's page or the [report form](/report). Say which ID, what you believe is inaccurate and, where you can, point to evidence. Reports go privately to the maintainers and are not published.

### What happens after a report?
A maintainer reviews it as capacity allows. This is a volunteer-run pilot, so there is no guaranteed response time. The maintainer may contact the registrant, and the record may be corrected, annotated, withdrawn or removed. While a serious concern is unresolved, a record may be marked "disputed" with a short neutral note. Marking a record disputed is not a finding that it is false. See [Disputes and corrections](/disputes/).

### Will the registry investigate whether a book really used AI?
No. It does not investigate how a book was written, compare texts, run detection tools or decide who the "real" author is. It records declarations and the challenges made to them.

### What if someone registers my book without my permission?
Report it. The Terms require that a registrant be the author or publisher or have their authority, and an impersonation can be corrected, withdrawn or removed.

## Privacy and data

### Is my email address published?
No. It is kept private and is never published, sold or used for marketing. It is used to confirm your registration, to send your private link, and to contact you about the record or a report made about it. See [Privacy](/privacy/).

### What is public in a record?
Everything you enter in the form except your email address is public and permanent. Do not put private or confidential information in any field, and use your author name exactly as you want it to appear.

### Who owns the data in the registry?
The registry publishes record data publicly, and the Terms say it may at any time release its records as open data under CC0 1.0. The registry's label definitions and marks are licensed under CC BY 4.0 and its code under the MIT licence. The [Terms](/terms/) and [Privacy](/privacy/) pages are drafts pending legal review.

### Does the site track visitors?
The site sets no cookies and loads no analytics scripts. It keeps limited technical data to prevent abuse and to give the maintainer a daily visit summary, without recording IP addresses in that summary. The exact details are on the [Privacy](/privacy/) page.

## For readers, librarians and reviewers

### How should I interpret a label?
As the author's or publisher's own statement about process. It is not a quality rating and not a verification.

### Can librarians and booksellers use the registry?
Yes. It is free and public, and ISBN lookup makes it practical for catalogue checks. Because it is self-declared, treat it as supporting information.

### Should reviewers mention a book's label?
That is up to the reviewer. A label is a statement the author chose to make, so quoting it accurately and noting that it is self-declared is the fair approach.

### Does a Human Authored label mean a book is better?
No. It describes process, not quality. Many excellent books will carry other labels, and many Human Authored books will be weak.

## Honesty and limits

### Can the registry stop AI-generated books being passed off as human-written?
No. It cannot stop false claims. It gives honest authors a public, permanent, dated place to make a statement, and gives readers a place to check for one. The absence of a record means nothing about a book.

### Is the registry biased for or against AI?
It aims to be neutral. It describes how AI was used rather than whether using it is acceptable. The founder is also an author and may register his own books, which is why independence matters. [Governance](/governance/) sets out the intended safeguards, including that no author, publisher, AI company or funder can buy a label or influence an individual record.

### How can I give feedback or suggest a change to a label?
Use the [feedback page](/feedback/) or write to [hello@ati-registry.org](mailto:hello@ati-registry.org). The registry is asking for feedback during the pilot.
